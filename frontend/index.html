How it works

Three steps, both directions, one call.

01

Recognise

Hand tracking follows 21 points on each hand while a trained classifier reads letters, signs and phrases — straight from your webcam, in the browser.

02

Understand

A dictionary check and sentence smoother turn raw signs into natural English. If a word isn't clear, SignBridge asks you to repeat it instead of guessing.

03

Communicate

Sentences are spoken to your partner, and their speech returns as captions and signing — over a direct, peer-to-peer video call.

Under the hood

Adapting to every signer

Each signer profile mirrors left-handed signing into the orientation the model was trained on and stores a few examples of your own hand shapes. At run time the model's predictions are blended with your personal examples — the Personalisation slider controls that blend, and uncalibrated letters simply use the base model.

Built-in signs and phrases

Both hands are tracked. Eight signs — ME, YOU, GOOD, LOVE, HOW, ILY, HELLO and YES — are detected from hand shape, depth and movement with no training needed. A small grammar reads them in ASL order, so ME → GOOD becomes “I am good.” and HOW → YOU becomes “How are you?”. You can also record your own phrases with three short takes each.

The signing avatar

Incoming text is planned into ASL word order: articles and “is/are” are dropped, words in the sign lexicon are animated as signs and everything else is fingerspelled, with motion for J and Z.

Checked before it's spoken

A dictionary decoder scores each word against the classifier's letter-by-letter confidence and word frequency. Only once every word resolves is the sentence polished — by a local language model when available — and spoken.
